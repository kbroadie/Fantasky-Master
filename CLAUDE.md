# Fantasky Master

Fantasy league for the TV show *Taskmaster*. Players pick one contestant per episode and score Show points (the contestant's score) and League points (5-4-3-2-1 by finishing place). Rules: `README.md`. Every calculation: `FANTASKY_MASTER_EXPLAINED.md`.

- **Live app:** https://kbroadie.github.io/Fantasky-Master/app/ (GitHub Pages serves `main` as-is).
- **Legacy site:** `index.html` at the root. It is frozen; don't read it or build on it.

## Layout

```
data/fantasky_master_data.csv   ← the ONLY league data (hand-edited; schema in data/README.md)
data/taskmaster_stats.csv       all-time stats for every contestant, S1–22 (imported, never hand-edited)
app/index.html                  sticky top bar (masthead + 3 tabs), the three pages and the footer
app/styles.css                  all styles; mobile-first, wider layouts in @media at the end
app/js/main.js                  renders all pages at load, tabs, swipers, sorting, series toggle, countdown, routing (#/series/page/arg)
app/js/ui.js                    shared helpers ($, esc, ord, framed…) and `state`
app/js/views/{table,episodes,cast}.js   one file per tab (Standings, Episodes, Cast); each returns HTML strings
app/js/podium-fx.js             canvas effects on episode podiums (winner's gold light, last place's stink gas), and the gold light on the first-place Cast header
app/js/heroes.js                presentation only: each series' cast photos (a group photo, or a hero photo per contestant), and each contestant's eyes and head size in them (Standings rows, Cast profiles)
app/js/league.js                pure scoring engine (derive) — must match the systems doc
app/js/csv.js                   CSV → series objects
app/js/alltime.js               loads taskmaster_stats.csv: the radar baseline, all-time record badges, profile facts
app/js/edit.js                  edit mode: picks and scores entered on the page, saved to the CSV through the GitHub API
app/js/wiki.js                  reads an episode's scores from the Taskmaster Wiki's API (for edit mode)
app/js/checks.js                the CSV's rules, shared by check-data and edit mode (which checks before saving)
tools/check-data.mjs            validates the CSV (checks.js) + worked-example regression + cross-check against the stats (no deps)
tools/import-stats.mjs          regenerates taskmaster_stats.csv from the all-time stats Google Sheet
tools/screenshots.mjs           Playwright shots of every view at 390px and 1440px → shots/
```

## UX framework

The user's v1 prototype is the model: fast, clean, obvious navigation. The aim is to make it fun and emotionally engaging without making anyone think harder.

- **Top bar** (sticky, frosted glass):
  - The brand.
  - The series number, a gold chip with a swap icon: tap it to switch series.
  - One line of text counting down to the next episode: "Ep 5 airs in 5d 18h" (no pill or background; the numbers are gold). The air date and time are on the episode page, not here.
  - Below them, three segmented tabs, with a gold panel that slides behind the active one.
  - On scroll it compacts to one thin line plus label-only tabs. The height it saves goes back as margin (`--hdr-h`, `--tabs-h`… in `:root`), so the layout height never changes. Keep that invariant, or scroll anchoring makes the bar flicker.
  - The sub-tab strips (Standings' Ep 1–10, the Episodes strip, the Cast names; `.page > .strip`) are sticky too (on request), right under the bar, and compact with it: their tabs shorten from `--strip-h` 44px to `--strip-hc` 32px, their `top` follows the bar's height, and like the bar they give the saved height back as margin, so the layout height still never changes (checked: the page height and the content below stay put through compaction).
- **Standings:**

The stage is a soft red light from above with the scoring cards' film grain (`--grain`), closed by a gold hairline that fades out at both ends (`::after`, like the footer's) rather than a hard rule; kickers and lines have a dark halo so they read over the Cast winner's rays.
  - The hero at the top is a kicker, "Series 22 · Thu, Sep 24" (that week's episode date, like the episode head's), then a headline, "Week 4 Standings" (`standingsHero` in `table.js`), set like an episode title, then one line with the leaders: "Riley leads The Show   Jamie leads The League" (the boards named as proper names, on request; names in gold, "The Show" and "The League" in their colours, `--show-hi` red and `--league-hi` blue; no emoji, scores or separator; "lead" for a tie, "wins" once the series is over, one phrase if the same player leads both). Under it a "How scoring works" button (`.st-how`: gold DM Mono caps with a chevron, chosen over the old dotted-underlined "Show"/"League" words, which read as decoration) opens both explanations, Show then League, and closes them again; it stays open through week and series changes (`state.how`). Elevated with texture and subtle light: a fine film grain (SVG noise, `--grain`), a pool of the theme colour glowing from the top that drifts slowly (14s / 17s, out of step) and shifts as the card moves up the screen (`--lx`, set in `parallax` in `main.js`), and a glint that now and then flares on the icon. Paused while closed; still under reduced motion. Columns are `minmax(0, 1fr)` and the rows are shared through subgrid, so every level lines up. Before the first episode it says when the series starts. No last-week result (removed on request).
  - Above the hero, an episode strip ("Ep 1"…"Ep 10", on request; `weekTabs` in `table.js`, the same `.strip` as the Episodes tab; weeks not yet scored are faint) shows the standings as they were after any scored week. A week not yet scored is headed "Episode 5 Picks" (on request; a scored one stays "Week 4 Standings"): the table as it stands now (no movement), each row behind that week's pick, and Patatas for anyone whose pick isn't in yet (so every week after next is all Patatas until picks arrive). `state.wk` holds the week (`stWeek` clamps it; `atWeek` gives every player's totals, ranks and movement at that week); the route is `#/22/standings/3`, and a new series opens on its latest scored week (Episode 1 Picks before the first episode, with when the series starts under it). - **Edit mode** (`edit.js`, on request: the commissioner enters picks and scores on the page instead of editing the CSV):
  - Opened by tapping all seven footer ducks. The first time on a device it asks for a GitHub fine-grained token (this repo only, Contents: Read and write), kept in that browser's localStorage (`fm-gh-key`) and never in the site. The ducks are a hidden door, not security; the token is the lock. While editing the ducks stay lit and a frosted bar sits at the bottom: status ("2 unsaved changes"), Key, Save, Done.
  - Standings: an opened row offers the five contestants and None for the week on show (`pickChooser`), replacing the ten-picks strip; the backdrop, totals and order update at once (`patchRows` in `main.js`, the same FLIP as changing week).
  - Episodes are edited in place, not in a separate form (on request). The title becomes an input set like the heading (`edTitle`, dashed underline; Enter or leaving it applies it). An aired episode's task table becomes inputs (`edTable`: names and scores, 16px so iOS doesn't zoom; the type icon cycles Prize → Filmed → Team → Live; live totals; "+ Task" and Tiebreak below it; a blank table of five tasks before any scores). What's typed is kept in a draft and applied when focus leaves the table (`commit`), so moving between boxes never redraws the page under the keyboard; empty tasks are dropped, and a task with no name or a score that isn't 0–10 or DQ is outlined and not applied. Save applies any draft first, and `refresh` puts focus back on the same box after a redraw (`data-fk`). Under the title a "Get scores from the wiki" button (before broadcast, "Get title from the wiki") fills in the table (`fetchEpisode` in `wiki.js`: the Series page lists each episode's page; its `tmtable` gives each task's type (Prize/Team/Live labels, "Team Live" counts as Live), scores, split-task parts (max of the parent and the parts' sum), bonus rows (added) and tiebreak ✔). The data file's task names and DQs are kept (the wiki often shows a DQ as 0); a placeholder title is filled in too. Before broadcast it fetches only the title, from the Series page's episode list (`fetchTitle` in `wiki.js`; the wiki lists titles before broadcast).

The result must pass `checkData` (the rules CI runs) before it's sent; warnings (a missing tiebreak) ask first. Rows go where a person would put them: scores right after their episode row, picks at the end of the episode's block in player order. `parseRows`/`toCSV` in `csv.js` round-trip the file byte for byte, so untouched rows never change.

- **Times are local:** every date, time and countdown uses the device's time zone and locale (`fmtDay`, `fmtWhen` in `ui.js`). Never hard-code London. Episodes air at 10pm London time (`AIR_TZ`, `AIR_HOUR`); `airInstant` turns that into an exact instant with daylight saving applied (checked: BST episodes are 21:00 UTC, GMT ones 22:00, so the week between the UK and US clock changes lands an hour later in the US).
- **Icons:** task types (Prize, Filmed, Team, Live) use one monoline SVG set (`ICON_PATHS` / `icon()` in `ui.js`): 16px grid, 1.5 stroke, gold. Don't mix in emoji; they render in clashing styles. Emoji are only for the 👑/🏆/crown badges.
- **Nothing smaller than 11px.** Use weight and colour for hierarchy.
- **Standings backdrop layers:** the photo and its reshade must rasterise as one layer (`.pc-bg` is isolated; the `img` has no `filter`, `will-change` or 3D transform; a filter goes on `.pc-bg` as a whole, as the frosted blur does). Each row's photo is thousands of pixels wide; promoted to its own compositing layer, iOS Safari would have to re-rasterise every one while the top bar resized. (These rules were first written to stop a white flash on the rows; the actual cause turned out to be the row's `:active` tint, now removed. Keep them anyway: they're cheap and keep scrolling smooth.)
  - Rows (`.pc`) must not be compositing layers of their own. Giving each one `will-change: transform` (tried, to make its overflow clip the backdrop on iOS) doubled the layers, and iOS then dropped rows' text after scrolling up and down. Instead the backdrop clips itself to its row with `clip-path: inset(…)` on its own layer (applied after the blur), which trims the bleed and the part below a closed row, so no dark glow shows past the row; an opened row's clip drops with the expand to its measured height (`--open-h`, `sizeOpen` in `main.js`).
  - `.pc-bg` itself is a permanent compositing layer (`will-change: filter`) so the blur ↔ sharp transition never promotes or demotes a layer (that switch made the colours jump on iOS), and `overflow: hidden` keeps that layer to the row plus its bleed rather than the whole photo.
- **Emotion comes from faces, gold and colour, not motion.** Use framed portraits, a crown for the winner, gold for first place, gold for a pick that won, and contestant accent colours. The only decorative motion is the episode podium effects, the Standings backdrop parallax and the drifting light on the scoring cards (all requested; see above), which stop under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Otherwise transitions are only for navigation and touch feedback, such as the tab slide, the row expanding and the sort bar's slide.

## Conventions

- **No personalisation:** there is no "you", player picker or planner (removed on request). There are three tabs only.
- **No build step, no framework, no runtime dependencies.** Native ES modules; `package.json` is for tooling only.
- **Views** are functions returning template strings. Escape user and CSV text with `esc()`, or `rich()` to allow `<strong>`. Interactions go through delegated listeners in `main.js`.
- **Contestants** always appear as full gold-framed portraits (`framed()`), never cropped circles.
- **Fonts:** the v1 set, loaded from Google Fonts, one job each:
  - `--ff-head` Bungee: headings and the brand. It has one weight; keep it at 400.
  - `--ff-name` Fredoka: player and contestant names.
  - `--ff` Inter: body text and every score.
  - `--fm` DM Mono: labels. Only 400 and 500 are loaded.
  - `font-synthesis: none` is set, so never ask for a weight that isn't loaded.
  - Use tabular figures for numbers.
- **Terms:** use **Show** and **League** points, as in README.md.
- **Imgur images:** the page sets `referrer: no-referrer` because Imgur blocks some referrers. Portraits load as `…m.webp`.
